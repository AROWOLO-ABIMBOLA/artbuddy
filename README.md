# Art Buddy

Fun art for children and teenagers, ages 2 to 17: a Paint studio with Mirror Magic, **Learn to draw** and **Learn to paint** courses (a Watch canvas shows each step and the child copies it on their own canvas, with feedback and stars), 51 animals to draw and colour (farm and pets, wild animals, water animals, birds and little creatures), a Colouring book of Nigerian pictures, Selfie art (turn a photo of yourself into cartoon, pop art, sketch, dots, pixel, indigo and neon art), a Make-me portrait builder, a Mix Lab for learning how colours mix, Bubble Pop, an Art Arcade with colour legends and crowns, and Art School for four classes with lessons, practice and certificates.

Made by **Dr. Arowolo Ayoola**.

## For parents: how it works

**Each child's progress and pictures are saved on the device they play on.** There are no accounts and nothing is sent to a server, so the game is private and works for children in different homes and locations, each on their own device.

- **Photos stay on the device.** Selfie art uses the camera only when the child taps **Camera** (the browser asks permission first), or a photo they choose. The picture is changed on the device and is never uploaded. In **🔒 Parent corner → Settings** a grown-up can switch the camera off.
- **Install it like an app.** On Android (Chrome), tap **📲 Install app** on the home screen, or the browser menu **⋮ → Add to Home screen / Install app**. On iPhone or iPad (Safari), tap the **Share** button, then **Add to Home Screen**. The game then has its own icon and works even without internet.
- **Please install it on iPhones and iPads.** Safari may clear website data if a site is not opened for 7 days. Games added to the Home Screen keep their progress and pictures.
- **Several children on one device:** tap **👪** on the home screen to switch child or add a new one. Each child keeps their own stars, crowns, stickers, outfit, gallery and voice messages.
- **Moving to a new phone or keeping a safe copy:** open the **🔒 Parent corner → Settings → 💾 Save backup**. The backup includes the child's progress **and their gallery of pictures**. Send the file to the new device (for example by WhatsApp or email), then on the new device use **📂 Load backup**. Voice messages are not included in backups; record them again on the new device.
- **Voice messages** need microphone permission the first time you record. They are stored only on that device.
- **Head start by age:** the Parent corner chooses whether some levels and the Art School class that suits the child's age start open.

## Learning to draw and paint (new)

**Two canvases.** In every drawing lesson the screen has a **Watch** canvas, where the lesson draws itself one step at a time (with a replay button), and a **Your turn** canvas, where the child copies the same step. On a phone the two canvases sit one above the other; on a tablet or computer they sit side by side. The child's canvas starts **blank**: the child watches, then copies from memory. A small eye button shows a faint hint for a child who is stuck.

**It checks and teaches.** When the child presses **Next**, the game compares their step with the lesson, says what it sees ("I cannot see the circle yet. Look at the Watch picture and draw it"), and gives 1 to 3 stars. A child is never stuck: after one gentle hint they can move on, and children aged 2 to 4 always move on. Every animal starts with a **Look** step that names its shapes (circles, ovals, rectangles, triangles, curves), ends with a **make it neat** step, and then a finish screen with a fact about the animal, its real colours, a 2-question shape quiz, and buttons to **Paint it**, **Colour it** or **Draw another**.

**Learn to draw (9 lessons):** lines, circles and ovals, squares and triangles, building a hut from shapes, light and dark (hatching), drawing a face (proportions), a 3D cube, perspective, and a shaded ball. **Learn to paint (7 lessons):** covering a shape with colour, staying inside the lines, big brush and small brush, warm and cool colours, painting in layers (sunset), the Nigerian flag (white is a paint colour too), and light and dark tones of one colour. Painting lessons are checked for coverage, paint that spills outside the shape, and whether the colour is the right kind (for example a warm colour for the sun). Every lesson ends with a short quiz and "what you learned".

**51 animals in 5 groups:** Farm and pets (12), Wild animals (10), Water animals (11), Birds (8) and Little creatures (10). Each can be drawn step by step and coloured in the colours it really has. Colouring pages check that the main body parts use a real colour for that animal. Facts were checked; several are Nigerian (elephants in Yankari, the Cross River gorilla, weaver birds, the agama lizard, Durbar horses).

**New trophies:** Animal artist, Zoo keeper, Art student, Shape spotter, Star pupil, Drawing graduate and Painting graduate.

## What children learn

| Class | Ages | Themes |
|---|---|---|
| Little Painters | 2 to 5 | Colour names, shapes and lines, mixing red, yellow and blue, light and dark, patterns |
| Junior Artists | about 6 to 8 (Primary 1 to 3) | Primary and secondary colours, warm and cool, tints and shades, pattern, symmetry, Nigerian art and craft |
| Senior Artists | about 9 to 11 (Primary 4 to 6) | The colour wheel and tertiary colours, value and contrast, perspective and shading, famous artists |
| Teen Artists | 12 and up | Colour theory, composition, light and shadow, art history, digital art (RGB, CMYK, pixels and vectors) |

Each class has 3 terms of 3 topics: **Learn** slides, a **Try**, a **Practice** round drawn from at least 60 different questions, then stars, coins and a sticker. A term badge for each term, a 30-question final challenge, and a printable certificate signed Dr. Arowolo Ayoola.

**Note on the curriculum:** the Primary content follows the publicly available Nigerian primary Cultural and Creative Arts (CCA) themes. The revised 2026 scheme of work was not available to check when the game was made, so please compare the topic list with your school's scheme and tell us if anything should change. Every legend and fact (the 17 art legends: Aina Onabolu, Ladi Kwali, Nike Davies-Okundaye, Ben Enwonwu, Bruce Onobrakpeya, Njideka Akunyili Crosby, El Anatsui, Yinka Shonibare, Monet, Van Gogh, Newton, Matisse, Kandinsky, Seurat, Frida Kahlo, Andy Warhol and Leonardo da Vinci) was checked against reliable sources; the characters and pictures are all original.
